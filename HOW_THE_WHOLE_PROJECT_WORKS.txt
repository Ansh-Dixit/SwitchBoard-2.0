1. WHAT THIS PROJECT IS ABOUT (PROBLEM STATEMENT & MOTIVATION)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In multilingual societies such as India, speakers naturally alternate between
Hindi and English within a single sentence — a phenomenon called **intra-sentential
code-switching (Hinglish)**. For example:

    "kal meeting ke baad main report submit kar dunga"
     [HI] [EN]    [HI] [HI] [HI] [EN]   [EN]   [HI] [HI]

Standard Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) models such as pretrained Whisper
struggle on code-switched speech for three specific reasons:
  1) **Acoustic & Lexical Disruption at Switch Boundaries:** When a speaker
     transitions from a Hindi word to an English word (`HI -> EN`, e.g.,
     `"kal" -> "meeting"`) or from an English word back to Hindi (`EN -> HI`,
     e.g., `"meeting" -> "ke"`), the decoder's internal language prior frequently
     hallucinates a same-language phonetic lookalike (e.g., transcribing
     `"eating"` instead of `"meeting"`, or dropping `"kar"` after `"submit"`).
  2) **Script Inconsistency (Devanagari vs. Latin):** In real Hindi–English
     corpora (such as MUCS 2021 Spoken Tutorials), English words are often
     written in Devanagari (`"मीटिंग"`, `"क्लिक"`, `"फाइल"`, `"ट्यूटोरियल"`) in
     some utterances and in Latin script (`"meeting"`, `"click"`, `"file"`,
     `"tutorial"`) in others. Standard WER tools treat `"मीटिंग"` and `"meeting"`
     as a 100% word error even though the ASR recognized the exact spoken word.
  3) **Aggregate WER Hides Boundary Failures:** Standard overall Word Error Rate
     (WER) averages errors across all words, masking whether fine-tuning actually
     fixed errors at the language-switch points or merely improved easy
     monolingual spans.

**SwitchBoard** solves all three problems by combining:
  - A **Unified Dataset Pipeline** with reviewed word-level `HI`, `EN`, and
    `OTHER` tags and 16 kHz mono audio resampling (applied to your 3,136-utterance
    **MUCS 2021 Hindi–English corpus** across 30 speakers).
  - A **Cross-Script Transcript Normalizer** that preserves raw transcripts while
    canonicalizing reviewed Devanagari/Roman spelling equivalents (`"मीटिंग" <-> "meeting"`,
    `"क्लिक" <-> "click"`) without losing reference word-to-language-tag alignment.
  - **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT LoRA)** on Whisper decoder attention
    layers combined with an **Auxiliary Language Identification (LID) Head** on
    decoder hidden states and **3-Stage Curriculum Learning**.
  - A **Switch-Boundary Evaluation Engine** that separates **Boundary WER**
    (words immediately before and after a language switch) from **Non-Boundary WER**
    with deterministic edit attribution and Code-Mixing Index (CMI) analysis.


2. END-TO-END SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE & MODULE BREAKDOWN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Below is how every folder and Python module in `SwitchBoard` fits together:

A. Dataset Preparation & Unified Manifest (`switchboard/data/`)
   - `switchboard/data/manifest.py`:
     * Defines `ManifestRecord`, the single schema used across all datasets:
       `utterance_id`, `audio_path`, `transcript`, `lang_tags` (`HI`, `EN`, `OTHER`),
       `speaker_id`, `accent`, `source`, `split` (`train`, `val`, `test`),
       `duration_sec`, `is_synthetic`, and `audio_sha256`.
     * `resample_to_16k_mono()`: Loads raw audio of any sample rate or channel
       count, averages channels to mono, resamples to 16,000 Hz using polyphase
       filtering (`scipy.signal.resample_poly`), saves 16-bit PCM WAV, and
       computes a deterministic SHA-256 audio fingerprint.
     * `validate_manifest_splits()`: Enforces four strict integrity rules:
       (1) `len(transcript.split()) == len(lang_tags)` for every utterance.
       (2) **Speaker Separation:** Zero `speaker_id` overlap across `train`,
           `val`, and `test`.
       (3) **Duplicate Recording Separation:** Zero shared audio paths or
           waveform SHA-256 hashes across splits.
       (4) **Synthetic Speech Isolation:** Any record marked `is_synthetic=True`
           or with `"synthetic"` in `source` is strictly restricted to `split="train"`.

   - `switchboard/data/importers.py`:
     * `import_mucs2021_hi_en()`: Parses your extracted `Hindi-English_test.tar.gz`
       (`data/raw/mucs2021_hi_en/test/`), reading the 30 full lecture WAV files
       and `test/transcripts/{text, wav.scp, segments, utt2spk}`. It slices all
       **3,136 timestamped utterance segments** (`5.19 hours` of 16 kHz mono
       audio), tags every word as `HI`, `EN`, or `OTHER` using lexical tables
       (never relying on script alone), and partitions the 30 speakers into
       disjoint splits:
         - `train`: 2,021 utterances (20 speakers)
         - `val`:   598 utterances (5 speakers)
         - `test`:  517 utterances (5 speakers: `mucs_834022`, `mucs_847066`,
                    `mucs_918821`, `mucs_921151`, `mucs_957491`)
     * Also includes importers for **IndicSUPERB Hindi Kathbath**
       (`import_kathbath_hi`), **Mozilla Common Voice Hindi & English**
       (`import_common_voice`), **Local Team Consented Recordings**
       (`import_local_team_recordings`, which enforces `consent_verified=true`),
       and the self-contained **Demo Fixture** (`build_demo_fixture`).

B. Transcript Normalization (`switchboard/normalization/normalizer.py`)
   - `TranscriptNormalizer`:
     * Preserves the verbatim `raw_transcript` and generates a normalized version
       for fair scoring.
     * Applies identical rules to both references and ASR hypotheses:
       (1) Unicode `NFC` normalization and zero-width joiner stripping.
       (2) Lowercase folding for Latin characters.
       (3) Punctuation stripping (ASCII punctuation + Devanagari purna viram `।`
           and double danda `॥`).
       (4) Lookup in `configs/normalization_equivalents.yaml` (containing 170+
           reviewed cross-script and Romanized spelling equivalents such as
           `"मीटिंग" -> "meeting"`, `"क्लिक" -> "click"`, `"बॉक्स" -> "box"`,
           `"सेव" -> "save"`, `"फाइल" -> "file"`, `"ट्यूटोरियल" -> "tutorial"`,
           `"कल" -> "kal"`, `"दूँगा" / "doonga" -> "dunga"`).
     * `normalize_reference(transcript, lang_tags)`: Maintains a `NormalizedTokenMapping`
       list linking each normalized word back to its `orig_index`, `raw_word`,
       and reviewed `lang_tag` (`HI`, `EN`, `OTHER`). Pure punctuation tokens are
       dropped cleanly with their tags so word-to-tag alignment never drifts.
     * Because both raw and normalized transcripts are scored in `evaluate_utterance`,
       the exact effect of normalization is visible side by side (`Raw WER` vs.
       `Normalized WER`).

C. Baseline & Comparison Models (`switchboard/models/`)
   - `switchboard/models/registry.py` & `switchboard/models/runners.py`:
     1) **Primary Baseline (`WhisperBaselineRunner` — `openai/whisper-small`)**:
        * Pretrained 244M-parameter multilingual encoder-decoder (Apache-2.0).
        * Used as the primary baseline so our before-and-after comparison uses
          the exact same base checkpoint.
     2) **Comparison Baseline 1 (`IndicWhisperRunner` — AI4Bharat IndicWhisper / `vasista22/whisper-hindi-small`)**:
        * Hindi-specialized Whisper model (MIT / CC-BY-4.0).
        * Transcribes embedded English words in Devanagari script (e.g., `"मीटिंग"`),
          demonstrating why SwitchBoard's cross-script normalizer is essential
          (reducing IndicWhisper's apparent error rate on MUCS 2021 from 18.99%
          Raw WER down to 11.04% Normalized WER).
     3) **Comparison Baseline 2 (`Wav2Vec2CTCRunner` — `facebook/wav2vec2-xls-r-300m` & `Harveenchadha/vakyansh-wav2vec2-hindi-him-4200`)**:
        * Explicitly checks whether the checkpoint includes a trained CTC linear
          classification head (`lm_head`) and character vocabulary.
        * Automatically detects and reports that base `facebook/wav2vec2-xls-r-300m`
          has `status = "requires_ctc_head_training"` (self-supervised encoder
          only), while supporting fine-tuned `Wav2Vec2ForCTC` checkpoints.
     4) **Fine-Tuned PEFT Runner (`WhisperPeftRunner`)**:
        * Reloads the frozen base Whisper model + trained PEFT LoRA adapter +
          trained Auxiliary Language Identification (LID) head, outputting both
          the decoded transcript and subword-level language predictions.

D. Whisper Small Fine-Tuning: LoRA + Auxiliary LID Head + Curriculum Learning
   (`switchboard/training/whisper_lora_lid.py`)
   1) **Freezing Base Whisper & Targeting Decoder Attention with PEFT LoRA**:
      - All pretrained Whisper weights are frozen (`param.requires_grad = False`).
      - `find_decoder_attention_target_modules()` selects **only** the linear
        attention projections inside the Whisper **decoder** (`self_attn` and
        `encoder_attn` `q_proj`, `k_proj`, `v_proj`, `out_proj`), leaving the
        acoustic encoder 100% frozen.
      - Trainable parameters represent `< 0.52%` of the model.

   2) **Auxiliary Language Identification (LID) Head on Decoder Hidden States**:
      - `AuxiliaryLIDHead(d_model, num_labels=3)` is a 2-layer LayerNorm + GELU
        MLP attached to the final decoder layer hidden states `decoder_hidden_states[-1]`
        of shape `(batch_size, seq_len, d_model)`.
      - `align_word_tags_to_subwords()` tokenizes each reference word $w_k$ and
        projects its reviewed language tag $t_k \in \{\text{HI}=0, \text{EN}=1, \text{OTHER}=2\}$
        onto all subword tokens belonging to $w_k$.
      - All prefix prompt tokens (`<|startoftranscript|>`, language, task,
        `<|notimestamps|>`), `<|endoftext|>`, and right-padding positions are
        masked with `IGNORE_INDEX = -100`.
      - The joint training loss combines standard ASR cross-entropy loss and
        token-level LID cross-entropy loss using configurable weight $\lambda_{\text{LID}}$
        (`lid_loss_weight`):

            L_total = L_ASR + (lid_loss_weight * L_LID)

      - Setting `auxiliary_lid.enabled: false` (`lid_loss_weight: 0.0`) trains
        **LoRA Alone**, allowing a clean ablation against **LoRA + LID Head**.

   3) **Three-Stage Curriculum Learning (`CurriculumTrainingRunner`)**:
      - **Stage 1 (`monolingual`)**: Trains on monolingual utterances (`CMI = 0`,
        187 utterances in MUCS 2021 train split) to stabilize base formatting.
      - **Stage 2 (`real_mixed`)**: Trains on natural code-switched utterances
        (`CMI > 0`, 1,834 utterances in MUCS 2021 train split) to adapt decoder
        attention to `HI -> EN` and `EN -> HI` transitions.
      - **Stage 3 (`all_with_synthetic`)**: Optionally supplements real mixed
        speech with synthetic code-mixed utterances (restricted to `train` only).

   4) **Checkpoint Saving & Reload Verification (`verify_checkpoint_reload`)**:
      - Saves `lora_adapter/`, `lid_head.pt`, `base_config/`, `training_config.json`,
        and `training_metrics.json`.
      - Automatically reloads the saved checkpoint from disk and verifies that
        both ASR logits and LID logits match pre-save outputs (`max_abs_diff = 0.0`,
        `reload_verified = true`).

E. Switch-Boundary Evaluation & Deterministic Edit Attribution
   (`switchboard/evaluation/boundary.py`)
   1) **Switch-Boundary Detection (`detect_switch_boundaries`)**:
      - Scans reference `lang_tags` for adjacent `HI -> EN` and `EN -> HI` pairs
        at indices `(i, i + 1)`.
      - Marks index `i` as **Before Switch (`pre_index`)** and index `i + 1` as
        **After Switch (`post_index`)**.
      - **Overlapping Switches:** If a word sits between two switches (e.g.,
        `"meeting"` at index 1 in `"kal [HI] meeting [EN] ke [HI]"`), it is stored
        in `boundary_indices` as a set element so it is **counted only once** in
        the pooled Boundary WER denominator, and recorded in
        `overlapping_boundary_indices`.
      - **`OTHER` Token Policy:** By default (`bridge_other=False`), `OTHER`
        tokens (proper names like `"Rahul"`, software names like `"Thunderbird"`,
        fillers like `"umm"` / `"हम्म"`, and numbers) are language-neutral and
        do not trigger a switch transition with adjacent `HI` or `EN` words.

   2) **Word-Level Edit Distance Alignment & Deterministic Insertion Attribution
      (`align_and_attribute_edits`)**:
      - Aligns hypothesis words $H = [h_0, \dots, h_{M-1}]$ to reference words
        $R = [r_0, \dots, r_{N-1}]$ using a Wagner-Fischer dynamic programming
        lattice that minimizes total edit distance $(S + D + I)$ while maximizing
        exact word matches (`MATCH`), with deterministic backtracking tie-breaking
        (`MATCH > SUB > DEL > INS`).
      - **Substitution (`SUB`)** at reference word $r_i$ assigns 1 error to reference index $i$.
      - **Deletion (`DEL`)** of reference word $r_i$ assigns 1 error to reference index $i$.
      - **Deterministic Insertion (`INS`) Rule**:
        Every insertion occurring at reference cursor $i \in \{0, 1, \dots, N\}$
        (after $i$ reference words have been consumed) is assigned to:

            attributed_ref_index(i) = max(0, i - 1)

        * Leading insertions before the first reference word ($i = 0$) are
          assigned to reference index `0`.
        * Interior insertions between reference word $i-1$ and $i$ ($1 \le i < N$)
          are assigned to the immediately preceding reference word `i - 1`.
        * Trailing insertions after the final reference word ($i = N$) are
          assigned to the final reference word `N - 1`.
      - Because every reference word index $0 \dots N-1$ is either in the
        **Boundary set ($B$)** or **Non-Boundary set ($NB$)** ($B \cap NB = \emptyset$,
        $|B| + |NB| = N$), and every edit is attributed to a single reference index:

            Boundary Errors + Non-Boundary Errors = Total Word Errors (S + D + I)
            Boundary WER     = Boundary Errors / |B|
            Non-Boundary WER = Non-Boundary Errors / |NB|

        If $|B| = 0$ (e.g., a monolingual utterance), `Boundary WER` is marked
        `"unavailable"`.

   3) **Code-Mixing Index (CMI) (`compute_cmi`)**:
      - Computed from reference language tags following Gambäck & Das (2014):
        Let $w_{\text{HI}} = \text{count(HI)}$, $w_{\text{EN}} = \text{count(EN)}$,
        and $N_{\text{lang}} = w_{\text{HI}} + w_{\text{EN}}$ (excluding `OTHER` tokens):

            CMI = 100 * (1 - max(w_HI, w_EN) / N_lang)   if N_lang > 0 else 0.0

      - Ranges from `0.0` (monolingual) to `50.0` (50/50 balanced mixing).
      - Utterances are grouped into 4 CMI buckets (`0 Monolingual`, `0-15 Low`,
        `15-30 Moderate`, `>30 High`) and correlated against WER using Pearson $r$.


3. KEY EXPERIMENTAL FINDINGS ON YOUR MUCS 2021 DATASET (517 TEST UTTERANCES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
On the 517 held-out test utterances (`6,559` total reference words: `2,852`
switch-boundary words and `3,707` non-boundary words across 5 unseen speakers):

  1) **Switch Boundaries Are 3.47x Harder for Baseline Whisper Small:**
     - Baseline Whisper Small (`openai/whisper-small`) has a **Non-Boundary WER
       of only 8.01% (297/3707)**, but its **Boundary WER jumps to 27.81%
       (793/2852)** — proving that code-switching errors concentrate heavily on
       the words immediately before and after a language switch.

  2) **Cross-Script Normalization Is Essential for Fair Scoring:**
     - `indicwhisper_hi_comparison` has a **Raw WER of 18.99% (1247/6565)**
       because it outputs Devanagari for English technical terms (e.g., `"क्लिक"`
       for `"click"`). After applying SwitchBoard's cross-script normalizer, its
       **Normalized WER drops to 11.04% (724/6559)** — a **7.95 percentage-point**
       artificial error reduction while still counting genuine word errors.

  3) **LoRA + Auxiliary LID Head Significantly Outperforms LoRA Alone at Switch Boundaries:**
     - **Baseline Whisper Small:** Overall Norm WER = `16.62%`, Boundary WER = `27.81%`
     - **Whisper Small + LoRA Only:** Overall Norm WER = `8.22%`, Boundary WER = `13.50%`
       (`-14.31 pp` boundary improvement over baseline)
     - **Whisper Small + LoRA + Auxiliary LID Head:** Overall Norm WER = `4.24%`,
       Boundary WER = `5.82%` (`-21.98 pp` boundary improvement over baseline, and
       an additional `-7.68 pp` boundary error reduction over LoRA alone!).
     - Supervising decoder hidden states with subword language tags (`HI`, `EN`,
       `OTHER`) directly helps the decoder anticipate language transitions across
       both `HI -> EN` (`27.67% -> 4.94%`) and `EN -> HI` (`28.26% -> 6.08%`) switches.
